About & making of

A fixed-step simulation (60 ticks a second, in logical rink coordinates) scores against a typed-array grid, never canvas pixels, so one seed and one set of inputs always earn the same grade on any screen. Self-tests replay scripted routes with the ticks fed in chunks of 1, 7 and 60 and compare every result, check that a bot driving closed laps gleams at least 95 percent of the scars on each of the next 365 daily rinks, and check that the best of three bot planners (skip-lane mow, spiral, racetrack) out-scores the plain mow. Everything is game scale: the rink is stylized; the machine, its mishaps and every number are invented. One pass has three zones (shave, wash, squeegee), a simplification of a machine that shaves, washes, vacuums and floods in one pass. As of 2026-10-12: the 2026-27 NHL season opened on 29 September 2026 (per nhl.com and Yahoo Sports); a resurfacing pass shaves a thin layer, carries the shavings by augers to a snow tank, sprays water and picks up dirty water with a vacuum and squeegee (per HowStuffWorks and Wikipedia); the 1949 Model A's four-wheel steering was cut back to front-wheel steering after it jammed against the boards (per Wikipedia and the maker's own history PDF). Seeding a daily puzzle from the date is common practice (per three craft write-ups listed below).

How it plays

Old Glossy, the invented machine, always rolls forward and cannot turn tighter than a fixed circle, so lanes need room. Steer with ← → or A D, hold ↓ or S to slow down, or drag on the rink and the machine steers toward your finger or pointer. Space starts and pauses, Enter starts a timed lap, Shift+Enter starts practice, R goes again, M toggles sound.

Every pass lays one stripe of water. Wet ice turns glossy and the scars under it vanish, which is a gleam. The tank drains with distance and refills in the door zone, the gap in the boards. With an empty tank a pass only shaves the ice, and a shaved stripe does not count. Bumping the boards costs points; tidy lanes with little overlap and time left on the clock earn them. A clean lap is a straight pass of at least three seconds, with water in the tank, over ice you have not touched yet.

Your lap ends on the buzzer, when every reachable scar gleams, or when you call it. The grade runs S, A, B, C, D. The result card has a postcard of your lap pattern that gives away no scars, a copy-text strip of ten squares, and a challenge link holding the rink, the grade and the distance. The same rink and the same steering always score the same on any screen.
